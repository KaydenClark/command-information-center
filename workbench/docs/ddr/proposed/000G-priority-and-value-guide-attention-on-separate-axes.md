# DDR-000G — Priority and Value guide attention on separate axes

## Decision

> Priority establishes the attention lane; Value informs the choice within it. CIC uses both to make useful recommendations.
>
> | Priority: when to attend | Value: return versus investment |
> |---|---|
> | P1 — Interrupt: stop normal work | V1 — Quick Win: high return, low investment |
> | P2 — Committed: current expected work | V2 — Strategic Value: high return, high investment |
> | P3 — Secondary: when committed work allows | V3 — Fill-In: low return, low investment |
> | P4 — Backlog: retained without current expectation | V4 — Defer / Eliminate: low return, high investment |
>
> The combined model has these rules:
>
> - P1 remains visible regardless of Value. Never collapse the axes into a summed score.
> - Value guides treatment within each lane. Quick wins get opportunity prompts; strategic work gets protected capacity; poor-value commitments get challenged.
> - Underlying assessments remain available: urgency, commitment, impact, investment, and estimates explain the labels.
> - CIC analyzes the picture: it recommends what to tackle, promote, reconsider, defer, or eliminate. Those recommendations become changes through the Owner’s command and the receiving system.
>
> One important implication: V1 should not continually crowd out V2. Your “protect meaningful capacity” rule preserves strategic progress while making quick wins visible.

## Why

The Owner explained that a genuine interrupt must remain visible even when its value is poor, and that strategic work requires protected meaningful capacity. The model asks separately when to work on an item and whether its return is worth its cost.

## Considered and Rejected

Adding P and V into a single ascending sort was explicitly rejected.

## Consequences

Display both categories with readable meanings and derive presentation recommendations. Exact scoring thresholds, estimates and classification authority remain open.
